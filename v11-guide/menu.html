Menu

Prices are in Canadian dollars. Tax is added at checkout.

1The menu is real text on the pageNot a PDF and not a photo of a printed menu. It loads fast on a phone, can be zoomed, read aloud by screen readers, and found by Google.Mistake avoided: a PDF that needs pinching and zooming, or a menu that is one big picture with words in it.
Vegetarian meat-free Vegan no animal products Gluten-free ask about the kitchen
2Dietary tags in wordsTags such as vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free are written out, not shown by colour alone. Many guests ask this first. Add a line telling people to ask about allergens (nuts, dairy, gluten, sesame).Mistake avoided: a colour-only code that colour-blind guests cannot read, or no allergen note at all. Allergen care is a safety matter in Canada.

Loading the menu... If it does not appear, please call us.

3Scannable categoriesEach group (Pizzas, Sides, Pasta, Drinks) has its own heading, so people jump straight to what they want. A name, a one-line description, then the price.Mistake avoided: one long list with no groups.
4Prices in one columnPrices line up on the right with a dotted guide from the name. The eye runs down the column to compare. Show the currency sign and tax rule once, near the top.Mistake avoided: "market price", prices buried in sentences, or tax surprises at checkout. Say "plus HST" or "tax included" clearly.
Order now Call to order
5A button at the end of the menuWhen someone has chosen, the next step is right there. Do not make them scroll back up to find how to order.
6Bilingual?In Quebec, French must be at least as prominent as English. Elsewhere, a French version helps many customers. If you add one, give it its own page (such as fr/menu.html) and a clear "Francais" link.
