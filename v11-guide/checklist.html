Basic elements every small shop site needs

Written for shop owners in Canada. Tick things off as you go. Words with a line of explanation are marked in the text.

iDesign notes on this pageThis page is a reference, so the notes are few. Turn them off for a clean read.

Jump to: how to test it yourself

Must-have Without these, people leave

  • Shop name and what you sell, clear in the first screen
  • Opening hours as real text, including holidays
  • Full address, plus a map link
  • Phone number that starts a call when tapped (a tel: link, with +1 and the area code)
  • Menu or product list as real text, with prices (say if tax is extra, such as "plus HST")
  • One clear main button: Order, Call, or Book
  • Works well on a phone
  • Allergen information for food: a short note plus "ask us"

Should-have Makes you look careful

  • Online ordering or links to the delivery apps you use
  • A Google Business Profile that matches the site (see SEO below)
  • A few real reviews or quotes
  • Photos of your own food and shop, small file size, with alt text
  • Links to your social pages, if you actually post there
  • Accessibility basics (below). In Ontario, AODA rules push businesses toward accessible service, and larger ones must meet WCAG (the web accessibility guidelines) on their sites.
  • A privacy line if you collect emails or names (Canada: PIPEDA is the federal privacy law)

Nice-to-have Add later

  • French version (required in Quebec for business, and welcome elsewhere)
  • Gift cards, catering enquiries or a mailing list
  • Short story about who you are
  • "Open now" badge and holiday banner
  • Newsletter or event page

Common mistakes Do not do these

  • Menu only as a PDF or a photo
  • Hours or address inside an image
  • Different hours on the site and on Google
  • Autoplay music or video, or pop-ups on arrival
  • Tiny grey text, or text laid over a busy picture
  • A giant hero photo that takes ten seconds to load
  • Prices with no tax note, or surprise fees at checkout
  • Tap targets so small that a thumb hits the wrong one
  • Letting the site go stale: old holiday banners, old prices

Mobile first

Most people find a shop on their phone, often while walking. So design for a small screen first, then let it grow on a big one. This site works at 375 px wide (a common phone width) with no sideways scrolling. Put the call and order buttons where a thumb reaches.

Contrast and font size

Contrast means how different the text colour is from the background. Dark on light is best. Aim for at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text (the WCAG rule). A free checker: search "WebAIM contrast checker". Use body text at 16 px or bigger. 18 px is kinder.

Page speed

Slow pages lose people. Keep images small: resize them to the size they show, and save as JPG or WebP. A phone photo straight from the camera can be 5 MB. That is far too big. Aim for under 200 KB per image. Skip heavy tools you do not need. This whole site uses no images at all.

Accessibility basics

Accessibility means people with low vision, limited movement or other needs can use your site too. It also helps everyone on a bright street.

  • Alt text: a short description for each photo (alt="Margherita pizza with basil"). Leave it empty for pure decoration.
  • Labels: every form box has a visible label, not just grey hint text.
  • Focus states: when you press Tab on a keyboard, you can see which link is selected. Try it on this page.
  • Semantic HTML (using the right tags, such as nav, main, h1 to h3, button): screen readers use these to find their way.
  • Do not use colour alone to give meaning. Write "Vegetarian" as well as showing a green dot.
  • Link text says where it goes: "See the menu", not "click here".

Trust signals

A trust signal is anything that tells a stranger you are real and reliable.

  • Real street address and phone number
  • Real reviews, with names or first names
  • Food safety or local licence notes if you want to show them
  • Clear prices, tax and refund or cancellation rules
  • Fresh content: current hours and current menu

SEO basics

SEO (search engine optimisation) just means helping Google show your shop when someone searches "pizza near me".

  • Title: each page has its own, with shop name, what you sell and your town. See the <title> of each page here.
  • Meta description: one or two sentences that appear under your link in search results.
  • Google Business Profile: a free listing for Google Search and Maps. Claim it, add hours, photos, phone, menu link and website link. Reply to reviews. This is often more important than the website itself.
  • Use the same name, address and phone (NAP) everywhere: site, Google, social pages, directories.
  • Menu as text so Google can read it.

How to test it yourself

The phone test

Open your site on your own phone, on mobile data and not Wi-Fi. Can you read it without zooming? Does anything slide sideways? Tap the phone number. Does it start a call? Try with one hand.

The 5-second test

Show the home page to someone for five seconds, then hide it. Ask: "What is this place? What do they sell? What would you do next?" If they cannot say, fix the top of the page.

Ask a friend to find the hours

Hand a friend your phone and say "Is it open on Sunday night?" Do not help. Watch how long it takes. More than 10 seconds means the hours are too hidden.

Keyboard and zoom test

Press Tab repeatedly: can you see where you are, and reach every button? Then zoom the browser to 200%. Is everything still usable?
