TORONTO — Fresh dough rises before dawn; the city rises to meet it.

Wood-Fired Pizza Declared "Rather Good" By Entire Neighbourhood

Residents report mass smiling, loosened belts and an unexplained rise in Tuesday optimism, even in February.

The oven at 42 Oven Lane has not cooled since opening day. Our correspondent arrived at noon and left at midnight, which he describes as "an accident, mostly cheese". Every base is stretched by hand, topped with tomatoes crushed that morning and fired for ninety seconds at a temperature the fire department prefers not to discuss.

Locals say the margherita alone has ended two long-running arguments and begun a third. The founder, asked for comment, simply handed our reporter a slice and walked away. We took this as a statement.

The paper understands the dough rests for forty-eight hours, longer than most politicians rest between speeches and considerably more productive. Charred edges, blistered crust and a generous hand with the basil are reported on every table. Even the snow, sources say, melts a little faster on Oven Lane.

Our Story, In Brief

Founded by two friends and one borrowed oven, Dough Re Mi began as a Sunday experiment: could a pizza be hand-stretched, wood-fired and still arrive hot? Three years and many flour-dusted aprons later, the answer is on your plate. We ferment our dough for two days, make every sauce in-house each morning and never, ever put pineapple on the front page.
